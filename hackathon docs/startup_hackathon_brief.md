Welcome to the ESF CoCo 2026 Hackathon, designed to encourage students to solve real-world challenges through creative technology and software engineering. Over a two week development window, participant teams will ideate, design, and engineer fully functional software prototypes aimed at making a tangible difference.

**Divisions & Year Group Mapping**

The competition features two distinct tracks: the Novice Division and the Senior (Advanced) Division. Your division is determined by your team's year groups:

* **Years 7 – 10:** Placed in the Novice Division by default, though teams may opt to compete in the Senior Division if they want a greater challenge.  
* **Years 11 – 13:** Mandatory Senior Division placement (any team with at least one student in Years 11–13 will automatically compete in the Senior Division).

**Programming Languages & Stacks**

You are free to build your project in whatever programming language or framework your team knows best. Whether that is Python, JavaScript, C++, or Rust (though please, for the sake of our judges' collective sanity, do not attempt to write an entire app in raw Assembly). The main ground rule is that visual block-based environments like Scratch or App Inventor are strictly limited to the Novice Division. Teams in the Senior Division must use text-based code. Just make sure whatever stack you choose includes clear setup steps in your repository so our judges can actually run your code\!

**Hardware Integration & Remixing**

Building physical hardware (microcontrollers, sensors, 3D prints) is completely permitted, provided it complements and enhances your software rather than replacing it. You are welcome to remix or modify open-source 3D CAD models online, but all custom mechanical modifications, firmware scripts, and wiring schematics must be documented and cited in your repository.

**Submissions & Evaluation**

Submissions will be evaluated by an expert judging panel across four core categories: **Innovation & Creativity**, **Problem Solving**, **Social Impact**, and **Technical Complexity**.

Each team must submit:

1. A **public code repository** containing all runnable source code, a setup guide (if applicable and the form of a README.md is preferable), and a brief AI usage disclosure statement.  
2. A **recorded 2-minute video presentation** pitch demonstrating your working prototype in action and detailing your development process.

**Live Finalist Showcase**

The **top 3 shortlisted teams from each division** will be invited to present their project live to our judges on **Sunday, 4th October 2026**  at South Island School during the main event to determine final podium standings. Teams unable to attend in person may present remotely online, and any physical trophies or awards will be sent directly to their school.

**Note:** The specific challenge prompts and overarching competition theme will not be revealed until the official start date. Join us for the live kickoff Briefing at 8:00 PM HKT on 17th September where the theme and prompts will be officially revealed. Further details will be emailed to all participants.
